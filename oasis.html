01 / Project Context

Understanding the design challenge.

For my Master of Science in Medical Device Engineering thesis at Keck Graduate Institute, I joined a six-student team, supported by a faculty advisor, to investigate engineering challenges relevant to ophthalmic delivery devices for Oasis Medical.

Our research considered how delivery-device geometry and fluid behavior affect practical design decisions, alongside clinical handling and usability considerations.

The project combined literature review, experimental evaluation, and preliminary design exploration. It provided experience assessing feasibility before committing to a more developed product concept.

02 / My Contributions

My role in the project.

01 / DESIGN

CAD & concept development

Developed preliminary mechanical CAD concepts to explore geometry, handling, and design feasibility.

02 / EVALUATE

Benchtop experimentation

Contributed to controlled benchtop experiments examining how physical and operating variables influence delivery behavior.

03 / ANALYZE

Analysis & communication

Reviewed test observations, considered engineering tradeoffs, and communicated findings within the project team.

03 / Engineering Approach

Testing to inform design.

Rather than treating CAD and testing as separate tasks, I approached them as complementary parts of development. Experimental work helped frame design questions, while CAD provided a way to explore possible mechanical solutions.

The process involved identifying measurable variables, supporting repeatable testing, comparing observations, and discussing their implications for early-stage device feasibility.

Working in a multidisciplinary student team also strengthened my ability to present technical reasoning clearly and incorporate feedback from different perspectives.

04 / Takeaways

What I took forward.

This project showed me how early engineering research can guide product decisions before a design reaches formal development. It reinforced my interest in hands-on medical-device R&D, particularly roles that combine mechanical design, experimental work, and clinician-centered problem solving.

This was exploratory research, not a validated or commercialized device.
