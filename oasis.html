01 / Project Context

Industry-sponsored thesis.

For my master's thesis at Keck Graduate Institute, I joined a six-student team working with Oasis Medical on ophthalmic delivery-device research, with guidance from a faculty advisor.

We looked at how device geometry and fluid behavior could affect delivery, while also considering handling and usability.

The work involved reviewing existing research, exploring design concepts, and planning and conducting experiments to answer specific engineering questions.

03 / Engineering Approach

How we approached the problem.

CAD and testing served different purposes. We used CAD to explore mechanical concepts and experiments to investigate how the devices behaved under controlled conditions.

We identified variables to measure, worked toward repeatable tests, and compared observations before discussing what they might mean for the design.

We shared findings and reviewed possible next steps as a team.

04 / Takeaways

What this project taught me.

What I liked about this project was that the design wasn't already settled. We had questions to investigate, and the experiments could change how we thought about the next version. I enjoyed being involved in that early part of the process, even when the results weren't straightforward.

This was exploratory research, not a validated or commercialized device.
