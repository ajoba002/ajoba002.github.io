Discreet Inhaler.

01 / Project Overview

A more discreet handheld format.

This project explored how an inhaler could integrate electronic feedback and actuation while remaining compact and approachable to use. The prototype brought together a physical enclosure, electronics, a pressure-sensing interaction, and a display into one product concept.

The goal was to investigate the experience of using a connected, feedback-enabled device, rather than to claim a clinically validated inhaler or drug-delivery system.

02 / Design & Integration

Bringing hardware and interaction together.

Physical design

Considered how the enclosure could accommodate the device's electronics, actuation components, and user-facing features.

Electronic feedback

Integrated pressure-based feedback into the concept so the device could respond to an inhalation-related input.

User interface

Developed display behavior to communicate feedback directly on the handheld device.

03 / Prototyping

Making the concept tangible.

Prototyping required the mechanical layout, electronic components, and programmed display to work as one system. The project was an opportunity to think beyond any single component and consider the full interaction between hardware and user.

04 / Reflection

Designing for the whole device.

What made this project interesting was the combination of physical form, sensing, actuation, and on-device feedback. It reinforced my interest in product development that connects engineering decisions with how someone actually experiences a device.
