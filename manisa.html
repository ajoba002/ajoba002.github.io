01 / Project Overview

One instrument. A cross-functional effort.

MANISA was developed through a Keck Graduate Institute medical diagnostics project involving approximately 50 students organized into specialized teams, including project management, marketing, software, hardware, and human factors engineering. I worked on the Hardware and Human Factors Engineering teams, took a leadership role within my team, and collaborated across disciplines to incorporate other teams’ requirements into the device design and user experience.

My individual contributions: Within this larger team effort, I drove the internal component arrangement, personally created the instrument CAD model, developed the seven-step workflow, and designed the user interface mockups. I iterated on these deliverables with input from teammates and other functional groups.

The design is a conceptual system. The case study does not represent a fabricated or clinically validated diagnostic instrument.

02 / Hardware Engineering

Instrument architecture & CAD

I led the development of the proposed instrument’s physical layout, personally determining component placement and modeling the overall system in CAD while incorporating requirements and feedback from other project teams. The architecture brings together sample preparation, robotic handling, reagent storage, processing stations, and access features within a benchtop enclosure.

My annotated design identifies features such as reagent holders, an Eppendorf tube holder, thermomixer, incubation chamber, magnetic bead separation station, pipette-tip and cartridge waste disposal, barcode scanner, power switch, and a shelf for sequencers.

I produced multiple views of the device, including a configuration with the robotic arm and a separate materials-and-colors rendering without the arm.

Design considerations

  • Internal component placement and packaging
  • Robotic access to sample-handling stations
  • Waste handling and reagent organization
  • Operator access and external interfaces
  • Integrating laboratory subsystems into a single enclosure

03 / Human Factors Engineering

From sample receipt to results.

As part of the Human Factors Engineering team, I developed a seven-stage workflow describing how a laboratory user would interact with the proposed MANISA system and personally created interface mockups to support setup and operation. These designs were developed in the context of the broader team’s diagnostic workflow and system requirements.
