MANISA System.

Hardware design, CAD architecture, and human factors engineering for a conceptual automated NGS instrument developed by a 50-person, cross-functional student team.

01 / Project Overview

Designing across disciplines.

MANISA was a Keck Graduate Institute medical diagnostics project exploring an automated next-generation sequencing (NGS) system for pneumonia and antimicrobial resistance testing. Approximately 50 students collaborated across project management, marketing, software, hardware, and human factors engineering. I worked on the Hardware and Human Factors Engineering teams, led design work within my team, and coordinated with other groups to incorporate their requirements.

My contributions: I determined the instrument’s internal component layout, created its 3D CAD model, developed the seven-step user workflow, and designed the interface mockups. Each deliverable was refined through cross-functional feedback.

Project scope: Conceptual instrument design; not a fabricated or clinically validated diagnostic device.

02 / Hardware Engineering

Translating requirements into hardware.

I translated subsystem requirements into a benchtop instrument layout, making component-placement decisions and modeling the proposed enclosure in CAD. The design integrated robotic sample handling, reagent storage, preparation stations, waste management, and operator access.

The annotated layout documents the intended locations of key subsystems, including reagent and sample holders, a thermomixer, incubation chamber, magnetic bead separation station, waste disposal, barcode scanner, and sequencer shelf.

The CAD views show different representations of the concept, including a robotic-arm configuration and a colored rendering without the arm.

CAD design views

Design considerations

  • Internal component placement and packaging
  • Robotic access to sample-handling stations
  • Waste handling and reagent organization
  • Operator access and external interfaces
  • Integrating laboratory subsystems into a single enclosure
Design decisions and CAD modeling by Anya, informed by cross-functional requirements.

03 / Human Factors Engineering

Designing the user journey.

On the Human Factors Engineering team, I mapped the proposed operator journey from sample receipt to results export and created interface mockups for instrument setup and sample operation. The work connected system capabilities with the steps a laboratory user would follow.

The sequence covers sample receipt, preparation, instrument setup, loading, operation, result generation, and export.
